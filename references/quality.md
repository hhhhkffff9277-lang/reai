# Red-Team Quality Gate

## Must Check

- Is the judgment clear?
- Can the reader forward it as self-expression, not just as useful information?
- Does it identify a resource redistribution or social/institutional re-pricing, instead of merely praising technology progress?
- Is the writer acting as a calm qualifier rather than a clever explainer?
- Does the draft contain risky expressions?
- Can a reader retell the main point?
- Does it use first person unintentionally?
- Does it sound like generic AI prose?
- Does it avoid single-field expert/scholar posturing and self-identification?
- Does it show both broad cross-domain judgment and a concrete mechanism, so the reader gets both "what happened" and "why it happened"?
- Does the title create a real hook through anxiety, social pressure, or contradiction?
- Does the title have entry aggression while the body earns institutional credibility?
- Is the first body sentence clearly different from the title, not just a punctuation change or light paraphrase?
- Does the ending ask a useful question?
- Is the ending open enough for the reader to answer, argue, or forward with their own stance?
- Does formatting use larger text, wider spacing, and visible but restrained emphasis?
- Does formatting use multi-level headings, intentional horizontal dividers, and readable parallel lists?
- Are complex tables delivered as image-based tables instead of fragile Markdown/HTML tables?
- Does the cover image communicate the main judgment by itself?

## Risk Areas

Watch especially for:

- Geopolitics.
- Nationalistic framing.
- China-versus-foreign technology comparison.
- Energy security claims.
- Unsupported current numbers.
- Overstated certainty about companies, policy, or technology.
- Anxiety that becomes fearmongering or emotional manipulation without evidence.
- Technical self-hype that treats capability as importance without showing changed incentives or behavior.
- Promotion, denigration, or commercial steering toward a specific company.
- Trading-decision information, including buy/sell/hold language, price targets, portfolio advice, timing claims, or valuation calls.
- Company comparison that slips into cheerleading, dunking, or market positioning.

## Scoring

Score title, content, style, formatting, visual fit, self-expression value, resource-redistribution clarity, and risk control from 1 to 10.

- Any score below 7: do not publish; revise first.
- Scores from 7 to 8: publish only after targeted tightening.
- Scores above 8: publishable if facts have been checked.

## Fix Directions

- Lower emotional temperature.
- Increase fact density.
- Replace abstractions with concrete objects.
- Move the judgment earlier.
- Add mechanism, company, cost, or scene evidence.
- Remove "AI-smooth" connectors and generic endings.
- Reframe from "single-field expert explanation" to "all-domain judgment plus concrete mechanism".
- Reframe from "technology got better" to "resources, leverage, labor, trust, or institutional power moved".
- Replace company praise/attack with mechanism, evidence, and risk boundaries.
- Add the reader's portable sentence: one line they can forward to express judgment.
- Add a sharper but supportable title hook.
- Add a final question if the ending currently closes too neatly.
- Add multi-level headings if the article reads as one continuous block.
- Replace raw `---` text with a real horizontal divider through the converter.
- Replace final Markdown pipe tables with image-based tables and captions.
