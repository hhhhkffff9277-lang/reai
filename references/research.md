# Research Workflow

## Fast Research Output

For an industry topic, produce:

- Industry status in one sentence.
- Three key data points or directional facts.
- Two or three representative companies.
- One core conflict.
- One counterintuitive point.
- Three title directions.
- One information-gap judgment: why this is hot or signal-rich outside China but not yet over-explained domestically.

## Process

1. Classify the industry and value chain position.
2. Extract market size or growth, cost structure, and technology path.
3. Identify representative companies and real assets.
4. Find a conflict: supply/demand, cost, technology, channel, regulation, or adoption.
5. Find one counterintuitive point that can support the opening.
6. Keep data scoped. Verify current or exact facts before presenting them as current.

## Information Radar

Use this when the user asks for topic discovery, daily ideas, "信息差", "外网已火国内没人讲", "选题雷达", or when a topic needs freshness.

Goal: find signals that are already moving in high-quality external channels but have not yet become a domestic consensus topic.

Scan, when available:

- Reddit: repeated user pain, surprise, backlash, workflows, community memes, early adopter language.
- Hacker News: technical skepticism, founder/developer adoption, infrastructure objections, security or cost debates.
- GitHub Trending and relevant repos: sudden stars, forks, issues, releases, README framing, developer enthusiasm, abandoned hype.
- Product Hunt: launch positioning, user comments, pricing reactions, category language.
- Bloomberg, The Economist, The New York Times, Financial Times, Reuters: institutional framing, macro/social language, companies named by credible outlets.
- Company sources: official blog, docs, pricing pages, release notes, careers pages, customer pages, SEC filings, earnings calls, investor presentations.
- Papers and benchmarks: what changed technically, what threshold moved, what limitation remains.
- `https://github.com/koala73/worldmonitor`: treat as a possible OSINT/news-radar source for geopolitics, finance, infrastructure, risk, and cross-stream signal discovery. Do not assume its feeds or scores are authoritative without checking primary sources.
- Domestic search/social platforms when accessible: use only to estimate whether the angle is already saturated domestically.

Filter with this sequence:

1. External heat: is there repeated activity across at least two external source types?
2. Domestic blank: is the Chinese-language discussion thin, late, overly technical, or missing the social/business implication?
3. Resource shift: does the signal change who gets access, leverage, margin, attention, labor, data, trust, or regulatory pressure?
4. Reader use: can a reader forward this to signal judgment or foresight?
5. Risk: could the framing accidentally become investment advice, company promotion, nationalism, or unsupported geopolitical certainty?

Prioritize "foreign discussion has a live object; domestic discussion has only a concept." A live object can be a repo, product, dataset, earnings line, policy clause, job posting, pricing page, complaint thread, or supply-chain change.

## Source Priority

Prefer:

1. Industry reports, such as IEA, EIA, BNEF, IRENA, BP, or brokerage research.
2. Company filings, earnings, announcements, and project pages.
3. Academic papers and journals such as Nature Energy, Joule, Science.
4. Reuters, Bloomberg Energy, Financial Times, and other credible media as secondary support.

Domestic sources may include official ministries, NDRC, energy bureaus, and major industry conferences.

For AI/tech/social-industrial topics, add:

1. Official docs, release notes, pricing pages, model cards, benchmark reports, and product pages.
2. GitHub repos, issue discussions, commit/release history, and dependency activity.
3. Earnings calls, SEC filings, investor presentations, and customer case studies.
4. High-signal community debate from HN, Reddit, Product Hunt, and expert blogs.
5. Credible institutional media as framing support, not as the only evidence.

## Paper And News Interpretation

Answer five questions:

- Which old misunderstanding did this correct?
- What is the most counterintuitive result?
- Why does it hold?
- What barrier or threshold did it raise?
- What does it mean for the industry?

Use this structure:

1. First screen: counterintuitive conclusion.
2. Scene, experiment, or engineering detail.
3. Mechanism explanation.
4. Industry impact.
5. Closing judgment.

Avoid merely translating abstracts, stacking jargon, or writing without an application scenario.

## Topic Selection

A topic should satisfy at least two:

- Involves real assets: power station, oil well, mine, pipeline, ship, factory, equipment.
- Involves cost change or price revaluation.
- Involves supply chain or geopolitical change.
- Involves a technology-threshold change.
- Involves resource redistribution: labor, attention, trust, distribution, capital expenditure, data ownership, compliance burden, pricing power, or institutional control.
- Lets the reader express judgment, not just learn a fact.

Avoid:

- Pure policy summary.
- Concept discussion without an asset landing point.
- Opinion-only pieces without data support.
- Pure technical progress stories with no social, institutional, or resource-allocation consequence.
- Company cheerleading, takedowns, trading angles, price-target narratives, or "who wins/loses in the stock market" framing.
